## What this is

Every ledger record is coded on six categorical axes by regular expression over
its own strings. Nothing is inferred beyond what the strings say; an axis with no
evidence in the record is coded `unstated` (precision, convention) or `none`
(carrier, killtype). Mutual information then asks a single question per axis:
how many bits does knowing that axis tell you about whether the record lived or
died? Because MI computed on 276 records with many levels is biased upward, each
number is shown against a 200-shuffle permutation null of the same data.

Two targets are scored. `outcome_5class` is the full label (win / pass / kill /
protocol_kill / inconclusive), entropy 1.3751 bits over all 277 records.
`lived_vs_died` collapses that to a binary over the 258 records that actually got
a verdict, dropping the 19 inconclusive ones. That binary is the owner's
question -- the information between what did not work and what did -- and it is
the table to read first. The base rate of living is 0.2442 (63 of 258).

## The convention governor

Round law, adopted 2026-08-19. Two kinds of move exist in this campaign and
they are denominated in different currencies. Conflating them is how a
bookkeeping correction gets spent as though it were a discovery.

**An EVIDENCE move changes what the ledger knows about a result that already
exists.** Re-pricing a verdict out of one currency into another is the type
case: the run happened, the numbers are unchanged, and what changes is which
wall the outcome is measured against. Evidence moves cost no compute, consume
no one-shot gate, and are reversible -- the prior pricing stays in the record.
Their risk is not spend, it is self-service: an evidence move must be legal
under a rule that was fixed before the result was seen, or it is just moving
the goalposts, and the write-up has to say which rule and when it was fixed.

**A DESIGN move changes the estimator.** Carrier flips and mechanism-family
flips are design moves: they produce a different program, so they need a run to
say anything, and the run consumes a predeclared gate under kill finality.
Design moves are irreversible in the ledger's own bookkeeping.

The measured basis for the rule is in the tables above. Convention -- the
currency a verdict was priced in -- is the strongest real single axis on the
5-class outcome and is null on lived/died. The ledger knows which wall a
candidate hit; it does not know whether the idea was good. So convention is a
gate a candidate reached, not a design knob a candidate can be turned toward,
and the adjusted_score cell's high survival lift is largely survivorship: a
record only reaches that coding after it has already passed cost, memory,
parity and legality. That lift must never be quoted as a reason to spend on a
design move.

Three consequences, stated so they can be checked rather than believed:

1. A kill made at a wall that has since been retired or re-ruled is
   re-priceable evidence, not a dead idea, and re-pricing it is free.
2. A design move may not be justified by an MI ranking on the convention axis,
   because that axis carries no lived/died information; and it may never be
   justified by an MI ranking on the carrier axis at all, which fails
   leave-one-win-out on a four-win ledger.
3. Recodes of this instrument are themselves evidence moves and are held to the
   same standard: every one is listed with its evidence class, the two general
   rules that were measured and rejected are recorded next to the four that
   were kept, and the doctrine agreement rate is reported over the untouched
   records so the check does not quietly become circular.
