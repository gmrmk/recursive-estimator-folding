# M137 terminal-law resummation: exact-four-moment escape hatch

## Disposition

**Killed as a terminal-law escape hatch.**  Preserve the certified interval,
normalizability checks, and target-cost interface.  Do not promote quartic
MaxEnt, an equal-variance two-Gaussian mixture, a Pearson/Johnson family, a
polynomial saddlepoint, or a moment-envelope midpoint as a replacement for the
existing terminal Edgeworth formula.

The important distinction is this:

* With **exactly granted** first through fourth moments, the requested ReLU
  mean is not identified.  This is a theorem-level information obstruction.
* The generated-network experiment is only a frozen numerical falsifier.  It
  uses Monte Carlo estimates of the moments, not free exact moments, and it is
  deliberately too small and degenerate to make a target-shape efficacy claim.

No contest files, targets, scorer, leaderboard, submission, or champion were
read or changed.

## Information result: four moments do not determine the answer

For every integrable real `Z`,

```text
E[Z_+] = (E[Z] + E|Z|)/2.
```

At the exact first four Gaussian moments `(0,1,0,3)`, both of these laws are
feasible:

```text
N(0,1):                       E[Z_+] = 1/sqrt(2*pi) = 0.3989422804...
P(Z=0)=2/3; P(Z=+-sqrt(3))=1/6:
                               E[Z_+] = 1/(2 sqrt(3)) = 0.2886751346...
```

They differ by `0.1102671458` (27.6% of the Gaussian answer) while agreeing
on all four supplied moments.  The second law saturates the lower bound in
the symmetric subproblem.  Conversely, take a symmetric law with `T=Z^2`
supported at `a=1-2/(b-1)` and `b`, where `b>3` and
`P(T=b)=2/((b-1)^2+2)`.  Then `E[T]=1`, `E[T^2]=3`, and as `b -> infinity`,
`E[Z_+] = E[sqrt(T)]/2 -> 1/2`.  Thus even under symmetry the valid interval
has sharp lower endpoint `1/(2sqrt(3))` and upper supremum `1/2`, width
`0.2113248654`.

This rules out a distribution-free claim that a one-shot four-moment closure
can recover `E[Z_+]` tightly.  In particular a Pearson, Johnson, or Gaussian
mixture family can select one member of this moment class, but its tail choice
is an additional modelling assumption rather than information extracted from
the four moments.  A deterministic selector has a worst-case absolute error
of at least half the displayed interval width on this shared moment vector.

For a general finite fourth-moment law the runner also reports the certified,
though not generally sharp, interval

```text
lower = max(0, (m1 + m2^(3/2)/sqrt(m4))/2)
upper = max(0, (m1 + sqrt(m2))/2),
```

where `mr=E[Z^r]`.  It follows from `||Z||_2 <= ||Z||_1^(1/3)||Z||_4^(2/3)`
and Cauchy.  The midpoint is a frozen minimax estimate only for this broad
interval, not a truth-calibrated closure.

## Candidate audit

| Candidate | Frozen selector | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Quartic MaxEnt | fit `exp(theta1 y+...+theta4 y^4)` only with `theta4<0` and residual <= `1e-7` | Proper in this generated screen, but fails the efficacy gate. |
| Exponentiated/titled Edgeworth | exact polynomial correction only when its density multiplier is nonnegative | The ordinary Edgeworth multiplier was nonnegative on only 25% of coordinates, all from dead networks; it is a numerical baseline, not a law. |
| Finite-cumulant saddlepoint | require a globally valid law from the truncated CGF | Rejected before scoring. By Marcinkiewicz, a nonquadratic finite polynomial log characteristic function is not a probability law. |
| Equal-variance two-Gaussian mixture | solve the two standardized `k3,k4` equations exactly, otherwise Gaussian fallback | Proper in this generated screen, but fails the efficacy gate. |
| Pearson/Johnson | no invariant moment-only selection exists without a family/tail prior | Not admitted: the exact counterexample applies to every such fixed selector. |
| Certified-interval midpoint | fixed midpoint of the displayed interval | Fails the efficacy gate by a wide margin. |

Quartic MaxEnt is a principled inference convention for feasible moment data,
not a proof that a fixed ReLU network's terminal law is maximum entropy.  The
existence/attainment subtleties for unbounded truncated moment problems are
real; this is why the implementation rejects nonnormalizable fits rather than
clipping their coefficients.  See [Ambrozie's truncated-moment MaxEnt
analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.7123).  The finite-polynomial saddlepoint
rejection is the standard Marcinkiewicz restriction, stated explicitly in
[Dinh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08469).

## Frozen generated-only falsifier

Configuration, fixed before the successful high-precision run:

```text
width=8, depth=32, 12 iid-He Gaussian networks
moment bank A = 2^20 iid inputs/network
stability bank B = 2^17 independent iid inputs/network
reference bank = 2^20 independent iid inputs/network
20,000 network-group bootstrap replicates
```

The reference bank supplies only a direct generated-network ReLU mean.  Bank A
supplies *estimated*, not exact, moments for the closures.  Bank B measures
the finite-MC moment instability.  Per-network grouped bootstrap treats the
12 generated networks as the units; output coordinates are never treated as
independent networks.

| Closure | Mean MSE vs independent reference | MSE / Edgeworth | 95% bootstrap ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gaussian | `4.162e-5` | `102.44` | `[37.65, 3090.54]` |
| Edgeworth `k3+k4+k3^2` baseline | `4.062e-7` | `1.00` | `[1, 1]` |
| Quartic MaxEnt | `1.984e-6` | `4.88` | `[1.176, 189.97]` |
| Two-Gaussian mixture | `3.744e-6` | `9.22` | `[2.310, 347.03]` |
| Certified-interval midpoint | `6.214e-6` | `15.30` | `[6.963, 434.52]` |

The predeclared promotion threshold was a 10x mean MSE reduction relative to
Edgeworth plus an upper 95% ratio below `0.2`, with a proper law for every
coordinate.  No candidate passed.

Finite-MC caveat, quantified rather than hidden: between the independent A
and B moment banks, mean absolute standardized skewness drift was `0.00886`
and mean absolute excess-kurtosis drift was `0.07129`.  The B bank is smaller
than A and is a stability diagnostic, not a confidence interval for an
exact-moment oracle.  Therefore the table does **not** prove the exact-moment
ordering at a target shape.  It only gives a reproducible generated falsifier
that is consistent with the stronger information obstruction.

Three of the twelve width-8/depth-32 draws suffered complete dying-network /
zero-output degeneracy.  Their terminal law is exactly a point mass and was
handled as such rather than allowed to overflow the moment bound.  This is
why no efficacy result from this screen is extrapolated to any wider target;
no post-outcome width/depth retry was performed.

## Cost boundary

`target_terminal_cost_interface()` reports scalar work *after* moments are
supplied.  The vector closure itself is cheap (Gaussian/Edgeworth O(outputs),
fixed quadrature or mixture solve O(outputs) with a fixed scalar budget), but
the interface labels moment acquisition `UNSOLVED_AND_EXCLUDED`.  It is not a
complete estimator or a way to evade the source-cost/accounting problem.

## Salvage map

Preserve:

1. `E[Z_+]=(E[Z]+E|Z|)/2` and the certified fourth-moment interval as an
   audit bound for any future terminal moment estimate.
2. Density/non-normalizability tests: never silently clip Edgeworth or quartic
   coefficients and call the output a moment-matched law.
3. The separation between an exact-moment information claim and a finite-MC
   generated diagnostic.
4. The closure-only cost interface for a future source that supplies stronger
   information, such as a certified conditional law or cumulants beyond four.

The killed link is specifically: “four terminal moments, without a separately
proved distributional/conditional-law mechanism, determine a competition-grade
ReLU mean.”  That does not rule out an estimator with genuinely new terminal
information.
