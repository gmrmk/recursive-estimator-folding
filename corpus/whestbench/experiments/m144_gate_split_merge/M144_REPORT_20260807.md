# M144 — gate-aligned Split&Merge Gaussian propagation

## Decision

**K=8 is killed by an actual full-kernel cost audit.  K=4 is not promoted or
given an accuracy falsifier.**  Its algebra and symmetry are sound, but it is
only a one-component-capacity mutation of the already falsified scalar
gate-boundary split, with no new causal premise for a material effect.  Its
literal local effective cost is also `0.591B`, leaving no reliable official
residual-time margin under the established tail-risk law.

This report contains no contest-target result, no public-score comparison, no
submission action, and no generated accuracy outcome.  The only execution was
a synthetic, target-*shaped* FlopScope cost probe and eight algebraic tests.

## What was actually proposed

For a retained preactivation surrogate component

`Z ~ N(mu, Sigma)`, let `S = diag(sqrt(diag Sigma))`, `R=S^-1 Sigma S^-1`,
and choose the gate-boundary direction

`y = (R + eta I)^-1 phi(mu / diag(S)),  a=S^-1 y,  eta=1e-6`.

The ridge is dimensionless because `R` has unit diagonal.  Standardize

`T = a^T(Z-mu) / sqrt(a^T Sigma a)`.

For the two fixed bins `T<0` and `T>0`, each has probability `1/2`, truncated
mean `+/- sqrt(2/pi)`, and variance `1-2/pi`.  With

`c = Sigma a / sqrt(a^T Sigma a)`,

the exact conditional *first two moments* are

`mu_+/- = mu +/- c sqrt(2/pi)`,

`Sigma_+/- = Sigma - (2/pi) c c^T`.

The conditional law is truncated Gaussian, not Gaussian.  M144 explicitly
replaces each such law by its Gaussian having these moments, applies the
noncentral full-covariance Gaussian ReLU map, and keeps the two child labels.
At a component cap, it merges each pair of siblings by

`p = p1+p2`, `m=(p1 m1+p2 m2)/p`,

`C = sum_i (p_i/p)[C_i+(m_i-m)(m_i-m)^T]`.

That merge preserves the first two moments of the **surrogate GMM exactly**.
It does not make the original truncated laws exact; that distinction is
essential.

The inexpensive deterministic cap schedule is binary growth through the first
`log2(K)` transitions, then binary split plus sibling merge at every later
transition.  It avoids a non-equivariant nearest-pair tie-breaker.

## Distinctness and symmetry audit

This state is not a moment-inferred two-Gaussian mixture.  It is seeded by an
explicit conditional event in the realized network's preactivation law.  Even
before a ReLU, its binary moment-matched surrogate has standardized fourth
moment

`E[T^4] = 3 - 8/pi^2 = 2.189430...`,

whereas the moment-matched single Gaussian has fourth moment `3`.  Thus the
split creates genuine retained non-Gaussian information rather than simply
renaming a `(mu,Sigma)` closure.

For a neuron permutation `P`, `(mu,Sigma)` maps to
`(P mu, P Sigma P^T)`, hence `R` maps to `P R P^T`, `a` to `P a`, and `c` to
`P c`.  For a positive ReLU gauge `D`, `(mu,Sigma)` maps to
`(D mu,D Sigma D)`; `R` and `y` are unchanged, `a` maps to `D^-1 a`, and `c`
to `D c`.  The standardized event, children, coordinatewise ReLU, and sibling
merge are therefore covariant.  The eight structural tests cover:

- exact recombination of binary truncated first and second moments;
- split recombination to its parent first two moments;
- permutation and positive-diagonal gauge covariance of the direction;
- positive-diagonal gauge covariance of the children;
- exact first-two-moment preservation under merge;
- the non-Gaussian fourth-moment witness; and
- the retained-history capacity witness.

All eight pass in the contest v0.14 runtime.

The literature premise is genuine: Monchot et al.'s ICML 2023 method propagates
a GMM using Split&Merge and a Wasserstein splitting criterion
([paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/monchot23a.html),
[reference implementation](https://github.com/PaulMcht/WGMprop)).  Its code
uses finite Gaussian splitting levels and a hard `max_split`, reinforcing that
bounded component growth is the central approximation.  M144 changes the
split direction to a gauge-covariant gate boundary direction and uses exact
truncated-normal first-two-moment identities; it does not claim the reference
implementation itself solves this competition.

## Literal target-shape cost audit

The prior `8.3/16.6B` estimate was incomplete: it omitted the repeated
noncentral bivariate ReLU map, float64 billing, dense direction solves,
elementwise special functions, and measured residual wall time.  I ran the
full array-operation skeleton on synthetic `256 x 256`, depth-32 arrays using
the installed FlopScope `0.10.0+np2.4.6`, float64, the existing validated
GL10 Gaussian ReLU routine, binary split, dense batched solves, and sibling
merges.  It did not load an MLP.

| state | retained / rectified components | billed F | residual wall | C=F+1e11 wall | C/B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| K=4 | 4 / 8 | 35.249B | 1.255 s | 160.747B | 0.591 |
| K=8 | 8 / 16 | 70.494B | 2.968 s | 367.336B | 1.350 |

For K=8, the actual bill decomposes as 34.427B matrix multiplies, 5.794B dense
linear solves, 11.822B exponentials, 3.234B normal CDFs, and 15.218B all other
array, merge, and quadrature operations.  Its F remains technically below the
272B hard FLOP cliff, but its combined-cost multiplier is already 1.350 before
real weights or an official tail margin.  It cannot be a low-multiplier
winning entry.

K=4 has lower local `C`, but the shared engineering record says local residual
timing can understate the official runner by roughly fivefold.  Applying that
existing safety law makes its residual contribution about 627B before its
35B billed arithmetic.  No production candidate may rely on the optimistic
single-run 1.255-second residual.  Peak live child covariance storage alone is
4 MiB for K=4 and 8 MiB for K=8; the batch ReLU working set adds many such
arrays (tens to low hundreds of MiB), so memory is not the binding issue.

## Bias class and adversarial width law

There are two irreducible approximations:

1. Each half-space conditional truncated Gaussian is Gaussianized before its
   ReLU.  It loses conditional cumulants beginning at fourth order (the
   explicit `3-8/pi^2` witness).
2. The hard component cap merges activation histories.  Before merging, a
   binary split tree has `2^ell` histories after `ell` gate stages.  A cap K
   necessarily assigns at least `ceil(2^ell/K)` histories to a retained label.
   This is a combinatorial capacity statement, not a universal MSE lower
   bound, but a subsequent ReLU fan can distinguish those histories.

For an adversarial rotating-gate network, each layer can present a fresh gate
direction to a later ReLU.  A bounded mixture has at most `log2(K)` retained
branch-label bits, while the fan supplies one new bit per layer.  Therefore no
depth-uniform exactness theorem at fixed K is possible.  In generic dense He
networks, the existing gate-tumbling observation makes the same problem worse:
the rank-`K-1` between-component mean subspace has expected overlap only on
the order of `(K-1)/n` with a fresh isotropic direction (`3/256` for K=4,
`7/256` for K=8).  This is a mechanism warning, not an asserted random-network
accuracy bound.

## Why no generated-only falsifier manifest was authorized

The recursive-estimator rule requires a changed causal premise, not a capacity
increment.  The pre-existing q=3 goal-aligned split had the same exact
conditional-moment/Gaussianization/merge mechanism, passed symmetry and cost,
and showed only a 0.2498% aggregate fresh-n64 reduction against its required
20% material-effect gate.  Changing q=3 to K=4 supplies no independent theory
that reverses that approximately 80x shortfall; K=8 additionally fails the
combined-cost class.  Running a new accuracy suite would be a cosmetic retry,
so it is forbidden by the premise gate.  No manifest was produced.
